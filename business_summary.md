# Business Summary — Q1 2024 (Jan–Mar)

**Food Delivery Business Performance Review**

---

## Overview

Q1 revenue fell **38%** (₹22,140 → ₹13,715), driven by declining order volume and average order value (AOV). The decline is heavily concentrated in **one region — Chennai** — where it stems from falling order frequency among existing customers, not customer loss.

---

## Key Metrics (Company-wide)

| Metric | Jan | Feb | Mar |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue (GMV, ₹) | 22,140 | 20,840 | 13,715 |
| Orders | 30 | 27 | 20 |
| Avg Order Value (₹) | 738.00 | 771.85 | 721.84 |
| Active Customers | 23 | 24 | 20 |

---

## Key Findings

**1. Revenue declined in both periods.**
Revenue fell **5.9%** (Jan → Feb), then **34.2%** (Feb → Mar). Both drivers contributed: fewer orders (30 → 20) and lower AOV (₹738 → ₹722).

**2. The decline is geographically concentrated.**
**Chennai accounts for 70% of the company's total order-volume loss and 52.5% of its total revenue decline**, despite being 1 of 6 regions.

**3. Chennai's decline is driven by frequency, not churn.**
Chennai's active customer count held steady (14 → 14 → 13). The drop is **falling order frequency per customer** (1.36 → 0.92 orders per customer) — not customer loss.

**4. Service quality does not explain the decline.**
Within Chennai, order loss is spread across restaurants, not concentrated in one outlier:

- **Pizza Hut** lost the most orders and has weaker service metrics.
- **Adyar Ananda Bhavan** — Chennai's top-rated, fastest-delivery restaurant — **also declined**.

Company-wide, churned customers had **higher AOV, higher ratings, and faster delivery times** than retained customers. Churn is not linked to poor service experience.

---

## Recommendation

**Prioritize Chennai.** The decline is concentrated there, and customer count is stable — the issue is behavioral, not acquisition.

Specific next steps:

1. **Investigate restaurant availability and operating hours in Chennai** — especially high performers like Adyar Ananda Bhavan that lost orders despite strong service metrics.
2. **Review Pizza Hut's service metrics** — slower delivery time and lower ratings make it a plausible contributing factor, though not a full explanation.
3. **Examine external factors** — competitor activity, pricing changes, lapsed promotions — during February and March.
4. **Expand the dataset** with restaurant availability status, marketing activity, and competitor pricing to move from directional leads to a confirmed root cause.

---

## Limitations

Current findings are **directional, not conclusive.** The dataset is small (30 orders company-wide; 1–7 orders per restaurant in Chennai), so per-restaurant conclusions carry meaningful uncertainty.

Critical variables — restaurant availability, promotions, competitor pricing, and marketing activity — are **not captured** in the dataset. The analysis narrows the search for the root cause but cannot close it.
